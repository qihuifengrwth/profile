About Me

I am a doctoral student in the Knowledge-Based Systems Group (KBSG) at RWTH Aachen University. My study is funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) under the supervision of Prof. Gerhard Lakemeyer.

My research focuses on knowledge representation and reasoning in multi-agent systems, with emphasis on developing formal and neuro-symbolic methods for epistemic reasoning. I am particularly interested in bridging theoretical foundations with practical applications in artificial intelligence.

I have collaborated with researchers from Germany (Aachen), the UK (Edinburgh), China (Nanjing), and Canada (Regina), and I look forward to further expanding these international and interdisciplinary collaborations.

Research Interests

My research focuses on knowledge representation and reasoning, particularly on reasoning about probabilistic and higher-order beliefs. I aim to develop formal and neuro-symbolic methods for epistemic reasoning in multi-agent systems.

Current Research Areas

  • Multi-agent epistemic planning - Developing decidable approaches that preserve expressiveness while bounding Theory of Mind (ToM) complexity
  • Modal reasoning and theorem proving - Translation-based theorem provers for multi-agent modal logics with introspection
  • Probabilistic reasoning - Expressive logical frameworks integrating probability theory with possible-world semantics
  • Knowledge compilation - Efficient representations for complex knowledge bases
  • Integration of LLMs with formal methods - Bridging subsymbolic and symbolic AI for explainable and trustworthy systems

Research Lines

Probabilistic Reasoning

Expressive logical frameworks that integrate probability theory with possible-world semantics, enabling reasoning about probability distributions in both static and dynamic settings.

Modal Theorem Proving

Translation-based theorem provers for multi-agent modal logics with introspection, preserving decidability while supporting first-order extensions.

Multi-agent Planning

Syntactic approach for multi-agent epistemic planning that bounds Theory of Mind (ToM) order to preserve decidability while maintaining expressiveness.

Neuro-Symbolic Methods

Integrating formal reasoning with vision-language models (VLMs) and large language models (LLMs) for explainable and trustworthy AI systems.

Future Directions

  • Extending multi-agent epistemic planning to broader reasoning tasks: logical programming for higher-order beliefs, strategy synthesis, and epistemic protocol synthesis
  • Applying theoretical frameworks to practical tasks such as social deduction games and human-agent interaction
  • Reasoning with probabilistic defaults provided by pre-trained neural networks
  • RL-based heuristic search for epistemic planning
  • Bridging formal reasoning with natural language via LLM-based semantic parsers
  • Exploring formalization of social science concepts (e.g., Jürgen Habermas' Universalization Principle, theory of communicative actions)
